**Saturn first, then everything else**

When astrologers talk about Saturn's harder transits, they often mention its passage through the first, seventh, tenth and fourth houses.
The first confronts us with ourselves and our body. The seventh puts the focus on relationships and the people across from us. The tenth tests what we have built and how responsible we have been toward our own path.

And the fourth house?
The fourth house can touch something deeper still: our roots, our family, our sense of security and the foundations we stand on.
From the moment Saturn stepped into my fourth house, changes began that I could not influence. Changes around family, the place I lived, relationships and everything I had considered safe.
And then came my father's illness.

I don't think anyone can be ready for a moment like that.
I remember my first thought was: "I can't do this."
I could not accept what was coming. I could not accept the possibility of loss.
But at one point I asked myself:
What can I do now?
I could not change the outcome. But I could be there.
I was the youngest child, and he often asked me: "Do you have enough? Can you manage? Do you need anything?"

And I did not want him to leave carrying that worry.
So I learned to be strong when I had to be. And then, once I was out of his sight, I let myself fall apart.
That is what Saturn can do.
It lifts you just enough to keep walking, and at the same time it confronts you with parts of life you thought you would never have to live through.

But Saturn in my fourth house did not bring only an emotional collapse.
It brought a literal collapse of foundations as well.
The house I lived in, the place I called home, ended up inside a project that put the whole plot through expropriation. We had to leave.
We had to move out.
And start life again somewhere completely new.

Only then did I understand that the fourth house is not just a physical house.
Because home is not only walls.
Home is memories. Home is smells. Home is habits. Home is people. Home is the feeling that you belong somewhere.

And when Saturn touches your fourth house, sometimes it does not just move the things around you.
Sometimes it moves the ground under your feet.

When I lost my father, I felt as if someone had stopped my life.
I asked myself:
How do I go on? How do I get used to this? Can life even go on?

During that time I was supposed to continue the third year of karmic astrology at the Johannes Kepler Astrological Institute. I wanted to stop, to give up.
I thought: "I can't go on."

And then I listened to a lecture that changed everything.
That is where I heard the sentence that stayed with me:
"Saturn first, then everything else."

Suddenly I understood that with Saturn you cannot run ahead and you cannot avoid it.
With Saturn, you get up.
You make your bed. You pull yourself together. You make a plan. You do what needs doing today. Step by step.
Discipline. Responsibility. Presence.

That was the moment I understood something important:
Saturn in the fourth house is not only a story of loss and family hardship.
It is the time when we build new foundations.

Only now, after transiting Saturn has left my fourth house, can I look back and see what I could not see then.

In my natal chart, Saturn sits at 0° Scorpio. As if the message had been there from the start: after a deep transformation comes a new beginning.
My Saturn also carries the symbolism of Pluto: tearing down so that something new can be born.

Today I understand that I could not control the circumstances.
I could not prevent the loss. I could not prevent the changes. I could not hold on to what had to go.
But I could choose how I would go through it all.

We do not get up the same after great losses.
We get up different.
Perhaps a little quieter. Perhaps more careful. But somehow more secure.

As I write this, I feel something I had not felt for a long time: security.
Security in myself. In my decisions. In my steps.
And the need to keep building my life on new, more stable foundations.

If you are going through your own Saturn in the fourth house right now and feel that everything is falling apart, perhaps foundations are being laid that nothing will be able to move again.

This text is not a rule for how Saturn in the fourth house must look. It is my personal story, my experience and my lesson. Every natal chart carries its own story, and every transit has its own unique way of showing itself.
